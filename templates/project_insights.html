Section 01

Project Overview

This is a final year project that estimates how well a YouTube video is likely to perform before it's even published. It combines natural language processing over the title, tags, and description with structured metadata such as subscriber count, duration, and category, feeding both into a convolutional neural network. The model outputs one of three performance classes.

Low performance Medium performance High performance

Section 03

Dataset Categories

Eight categories were sampled at roughly 3,000 videos each during collection. The deployed model (V2) was ultimately trained on the four categories with the most consistent metadata quality and the clearest performance separation — narrowing scope in exchange for a more reliable signal, rather than spreading the model thin across every category.

Music ~3,000 Gaming ~3,000 Sports ~3,000 Entertainment ~3,000 News & Politics ~3,000 People & Blogs ~3,000 Education ~3,000 Science & Technology ~3,000

Section 08

Model Development

Two baseline models were trained and compared before the final CNN was selected as the deployed model.

Baseline

Logistic Regression

A linear baseline trained over the scaled metadata features, used as a lower bound for comparison.

Baseline

Random Forest

A stronger non-linear baseline capable of capturing feature interactions without deep learning.

Deployed

CNN V2

A multi-input late-fusion network combining a text branch with a metadata branch, selected for its accuracy advantage.

Section 09

Evaluation

Model selection was informed by exploratory data analysis, a Chi-square test of independence between category and performance class, accuracy comparisons across all three candidate models, confusion matrices for per-class behavior, and training/validation curves to confirm convergence without overfitting.

Section 10

Challenges

Several challenges were encountered during development, requiring iterative improvements in data preparation, feature design, and model optimization.

Overfitting
↓ addressed through
Dropout layers, early stopping, and monitoring validation performance to improve model generalization.
Class separation difficulty
↓ addressed through
Dataset balancing and improved category selection to create clearer performance patterns across Low / Medium / High classes.
Weak metadata contribution
↓ addressed through
Feature engineering and scaling to improve the usefulness of subscriber count, duration, and category information alongside text features.

Future Improvements

Directions that would meaningfully extend the project beyond its current scope.
